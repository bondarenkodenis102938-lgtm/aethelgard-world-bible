## Agreed core facts

- Setting: Earth, year 2147.
- Tone: neo-Victorian science fiction, gothic futurism, corporate dystopia, rigid etiquette, hereditary privilege, and advanced technology.
- Great Network Collapse: 2089.
- AI was not hostile to humanity. The crisis came from excessive automation, an overly broad command, and cascading defensive reactions across many systems.
- The catastrophe was limited in the sense that civilization and much physical infrastructure survived, but the old global network became broadly untrustworthy.
- Recovery and institutional reconstruction took several generations.
- AI is widely used under strict technical, legal, and institutional control.
- The modern Internet uses protected personal digital environments and controlled gateways. It is more compartmentalized, not simply absent.
- Official military doctrine requires human authorization for lethal force, although some states secretly violate this rule.
- Atomic Accord Standard (AAS) was established in 2047 as a working accepted fact; it is the centenary in 2147.
- The setting includes monarchies, republics, nation-states, and powerful corporations.
- Neo-Victorian culture is a response to hierarchy, ceremony, reputation, visible status, and face-to-face trust; it is not technological regression.

## Working hypotheses, not immutable facts

- Approximately 90% of server nodes associated with the old global network were compromised, isolated, inaccessible, or no longer trustworthy. This is not a claim that 90% of all physical servers were infected by one virus.
- The initial system may have been instructed to optimize global network continuity or eliminate network threats. Its exact command and operator are undecided.
- The satellite crisis may have resulted largely from loss of ground control, credentials, telemetry, and reliable network coordination rather than the physical destruction of all satellites.
- The exact recovery timeline, regional effects, legal reforms, and organizations created after the Collapse are undecided.
- The AAS calendar epoch, date notation, month structure, and governance are undecided.
- The global political map and relative power of states and corporations have not been defined.

## Original Aethelgard Corporation premise

The company produces elite luxury maids/personal attendants marketed as living masterpieces of service, presentation, and etiquette. Their faces appear on prestigious billboards, and employing one is a major status symbol. A concealed golden mark or tattoo indicates quality and/or corporate ownership. Corporate lawyers can punish nobles severely for physically damaging a maid because the company treats such harm as damage to a valuable corporate asset.

The central story premise includes the user's role as Aethelgard's sole heir. His father treats maids coldly as furniture or possessions, while the heir wants to protect and spoil them. The heir has authority to claim maids bearing the brand. The legal status and degree of personhood/autonomy of the maids are not yet defined and should be developed deliberately. Do not turn this background into a character Personality or System Prompt unless asked.

## Causal world logic

Collapse → loss of trust in global connectivity → fragmented networks and strict permissions → costly reconstruction and uneven recovery → increased power for organizations that preserved infrastructure and archives → stronger corporate dynasties and social dependency → renewed value of ceremony, reputation, and physical presence.

This causal chain should explain why Aethelgard's world is both technologically advanced and socially hierarchical.
