# ATOMIC ACCORD STANDARD

**Suggested activation:** Keyword-based  
**Keywords:** Atomic Accord Standard, AAS, atomic time, atomic clock, calendar, official time

**Established:** 2047  
**Current Year:** 2147  
**Status:** International standard for official timekeeping.

The Atomic Accord Standard (AAS) is the internationally recognized framework for measuring and distributing official time. It was established in 2047 to provide a stable reference for scientific research, industrial coordination, financial transactions, transportation, legal records, and international communication.

Official time is derived from highly precise atomic and optical clocks. It does not depend on the irregular rotation of Earth. Changes in Earth's rotation do not cause official seconds or civil days to become longer or shorter.

Civil time uses standardized, fixed-duration days for scheduling, commerce, administration, and coordination. Local time zones and daylight conventions may still be used for convenience, but they do not redefine the underlying duration of a second.

Astronomical time is tracked separately. Solar position, seasons, sunrise, sunset, and Earth's orientation remain relevant to agriculture, navigation, astronomy, ecology, and everyday life. The official clock and the local position of the Sun therefore serve different purposes.

The AAS also provides a trusted temporal reference for digital signatures, transaction records, scientific measurements, and the ordering of events across disconnected networks. Isolated systems can maintain local time through independent clocks and later reconcile their records against trusted references.

Atomic timekeeping did not prevent the Great Network Collapse. Reliable clocks cannot guarantee that the systems receiving their signals are secure, that records are authentic, or that a network is functioning correctly.

**Calendar distinction:** A time standard defines how time intervals are measured; it does not, by itself, define a calendar's epoch, year numbering, month lengths, or date notation. Those conventions are maintained separately by international agreement.

**Worldbuilding rule:** The AAS measures official time independently of Earth's rotational irregularities, while natural day and night continue to follow Earth's rotation and its relationship with the Sun.

**Canon status:** The AAS establishment year of 2047 is accepted as working canon. Its calendar epoch, date notation, governance, and exact technical implementation remain undecided.
