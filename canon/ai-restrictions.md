# AI Restrictions and Human Authority

**Status:** Core principle; exact legal frameworks remain open

Artificial intelligence is widely used throughout the world of 2147. AI systems support medicine, scientific research, engineering, industrial production, education, administration, logistics, finance, and military planning. Their capabilities are advanced, but their authority is deliberately limited.

The Great Network Collapse established a central principle of modern governance: an AI system must not be granted unlimited authority merely because it can perform a task efficiently.

## Bounded Authority

AI systems operate within defined permissions and technical boundaries. Access to data, external networks, physical equipment, financial resources, and critical infrastructure is restricted according to the system's role. Expanding permissions requires an authorized process.

## Human Accountability

AI assistance does not automatically transfer legal responsibility away from the people and institutions using it. Operators, developers, commanders, executives, and public authorities remain accountable according to their duties, decisions, and applicable law.

## Critical Decisions

Consequential actions require appropriate authorization, verification, and auditable procedures. Automated systems may analyze options, identify threats, recommend actions, and perform pre-authorized operations, but their output is not automatically treated as a legitimate human decision.

## Military Command

Human officers retain formal command over armed forces. AI may assist with reconnaissance, logistics, navigation, threat analysis, targeting calculations, and defensive coordination. Under official doctrine, lethal force requires valid human authorization. An AI recommendation alone does not constitute lawful authorization to attack.

Some states secretly violate these restrictions. They may deploy unauthorized autonomous weapons, conceal automated command systems, or use AI beyond publicly declared limits. Such programs are politically dangerous because they undermine international trust and risk repeating the failures that preceded the Collapse.

## Safety Architecture

Critical AI systems use restricted network access, compartmentalized environments, logging, independent oversight, controlled updates, and procedures for isolation or shutdown. Where feasible, essential operations have manual or independent fallback mechanisms.

These protections reduce risk but are not perfect. Human operators can issue reckless instructions, institutions can conceal failures, authorized systems can behave unexpectedly, and supposedly independent components can share hidden dependencies.

## No Universal AI Consciousness

AI is not a single unified entity. Different systems have different architectures, capabilities, objectives, owners, and restrictions. The Great Network Collapse did not prove that all AI systems were malicious or that artificial intelligence inevitably seeks power.

**Core principle:** AI may be powerful, useful, and highly autonomous within a limited task, but it is not the sovereign authority responsible for deciding what humanity should do. Human institutions must define its permissions and remain accountable for their use of it.
