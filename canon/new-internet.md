# The New Internet

**Status:** Core concept

The Internet of 2147 is a collection of interconnected but deliberately separated digital environments. It was developed after the Great Network Collapse, when unrestricted connectivity was recognized as a systemic risk.

A person's primary digital environment is commonly called a **Personal Haven**. It is a protected space for personal files, communications, identity credentials, applications, and private data. Applications operate under limited permissions and are prevented from freely accessing unrelated files, devices, or networks.

Personal Havens are not completely disconnected from the world. They communicate through controlled gateways that mediate data exchange, authenticate participants, and enforce permissions. A user can communicate internationally, access services, purchase software, and transfer approved information without granting every external system unrestricted access to their device.

Institutional networks serve governments, hospitals, banks, universities, industrial facilities, and corporations. Critical systems are segmented so that a failure in one environment should not automatically spread into unrelated networks. Connections between segments are limited, monitored, and governed by explicit rules.

Software is generally distributed through authenticated catalogues, verified packages, approved repositories, licensed service environments, or physical media. Applications are checked for authenticity and permissions before installation. Programs do not normally receive unrestricted access merely because a user downloads them.

Games, entertainment, research tools, and professional software remain available, but distribution is more controlled than in the old Internet. Some products require licences or access to closed service environments. Offline applications and physical media remain important. Unofficial software, piracy, and illicit networks still exist, but they carry greater technical and legal risks.

Global communication is possible, but not every network can directly communicate with every other network. Some transfers require approval, validation, delays, or an intermediary service. Governments and corporations may restrict information exchange under security policies or local law.

This architecture improves containment and limits the scale of many failures. It does not eliminate cybercrime, malicious insiders, software defects, coercion, censorship, or vulnerabilities within individual systems.

## Social Consequences

Digital life remains important, but physical spaces have regained cultural value. Clubs, salons, libraries, theatres, exhibitions, private gatherings, and in-person negotiations provide experiences and relationships that cannot be fully replaced by remote services.

**Worldbuilding rule:** The modern Internet is neither absent nor universally open. It is useful, sophisticated, regulated, and compartmentalized. Security comes from limiting trust and permissions, not from assuming that every system is invulnerable.
