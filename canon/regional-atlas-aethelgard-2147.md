# Regional Atlas: Aethelgard and the Alpine Region, 2147

**Status:** Working map proposal. The geography choices below were selected by the author; most labels and political details visible on the generated concept map remain unapproved proposals.  
**Purpose:** Reference for worldbuilding and future bot/lorebook use. This document describes the current concept map, not a finalized geopolitical canon.

## Author-selected geographic anchors

- The region occupies the recognizable general location of the former Principality of Liechtenstein, but cities, valleys, infrastructure, and borders have changed substantially.
- The capital of the Alpine Confederation is in the Eastern Arc.
- Aethelgard's corporate core is on a high plateau above the surrounding valleys.

These are the only geographic choices in this document treated as selected by the author. They do not establish every border or place name shown in the concept map.

## Reading the concept map

The generated map depicts a mountainous Central European region in 2147. A golden, irregular territory marks Aethelgard's corporate enclave in the central-western mountains. A high plateau is labelled **Highland Keep**, with **Vaduz** shown below it in a lower valley. The Alpine Confederation occupies the large mountain territory to the east and northeast; its proposed capital, **Edelberg**, is marked with a star in the Eastern Arc.

The surrounding territories shown are:
- **Rhine Defense Alliance** to the northwest.
- **Free Communes League** to the west and southwest.
- **Rheinland Province** farther southwest.
- **Southern Marches** south of the central valleys.
- **Eastern Duchy** east and southeast of Aethelgard.
- **Tyrolian Domain** at the far eastern edge.
- **Other States / Territories** beyond the displayed borders.

The map uses shaded mountain relief, blue rivers and Lake Walen, coloured political boundaries, and multiple road, rail, and trade routes. A scale bar suggests a regional map rather than a continental one. An inset shows the region's approximate position in Europe.

## Borders and territories: map interpretation

- **Aethelgard Corporate Territory:** A compact gold-highlighted enclave centred on the high plateau and adjacent mountain approaches. The image implies a defensible core with access to surrounding transport valleys. Exact sovereignty, border geometry, and legal rights remain undecided.
- **Alpine Confederation:** The largest neighbouring polity on the map, covering the eastern and northeastern highlands. Its capital is placed in the Eastern Arc, as selected by the author; the name **Edelberg** is only a proposal.
- **Free Communes League:** A patchwork-like western region, suggesting several towns and trade routes that resist absorption by either state or corporation. The precise constitutional arrangement is undecided.
- **Rhine Defense Alliance:** An industrial and security-oriented northern neighbour. Its existence and exact name are proposals.
- **Eastern Duchy:** A territory east of the corporate core, positioned along alternative supply routes. Its government and relationship to the Confederation are undecided.
- **Southern Marches:** A southern agricultural and trade region near the principal mountain crossing. The map does not define whether it is a sovereign state, confederated region, or alliance.
- **Rheinland Province** and **Tyrolian Domain:** Peripheral neighbours used to frame the map. Their status and names are proposals.

The map's colour boundaries are schematic, not surveyed borders. It should not be used to infer precise distances, legal jurisdiction, or military control.

## Transport corridors shown

The map visually implies several route systems; these are not yet approved as named canon:

1. **Northern corridor:** Connects the northern industrial zone around Nordheim and Bregenz with the central Alpine valleys. It appears to combine rail, road, and river-valley routes.
2. **Western corridor:** Links the Free Communes League and settlements around Bludenz / Montafon to the central region.
3. **Central valley corridor:** Runs through the lowland settlements around Vaduz, Schaan, Buchs, and Triesen. This is the most direct approach to the Aethelgard plateau, but steep terrain makes the final ascent controllable.
4. **Eastern corridor:** Follows the valleys and transport junctions toward Sargans and the Eastern Duchy, then onward to the Confederation's eastern territory and its proposed capital.
5. **Southern corridor:** Runs through the Southern Marches toward the mountain passes and routes in the direction of Innsbruck. It is a plausible trade and logistics connection, but the exact pass and its name are not fixed.
6. **Mountain links:** Tunnels and passes connect otherwise separated valleys. The map indicates such links symbolically but does not specify tunnel count, capacity, ownership, or year-round reliability.
7. **Air links:** Small aviation symbols appear east of the central region. Their exact location, type, and operator are undecided.

### Strategic implication

Aethelgard's high-plateau location gives the corporate core natural defensive advantages and visibility over approaches, but also makes it dependent on valley access for heavy freight, staffing, food, energy equipment, and routine travel. The corporation can influence traffic through contracts, infrastructure ownership, and security agreements without necessarily controlling every road or the surrounding state's sovereignty. This is a planning inference, not yet canon.

## Recommended naming policy

To avoid accidentally canonizing image-generation inventions, keep three statuses:
- **Author-selected:** former Liechtenstein's recognizable general location; substantially changed geography; Confederation capital in the Eastern Arc; Aethelgard core on a high plateau.
- **Proposed labels:** Edelberg, Highland Keep, Lake Walen as a future map label, and all named neighbouring polities unless separately approved.
- **Unresolved:** exact borders, river names, official names of corridors and passes, state succession, transport ownership, distances, and the legal status of the corporate enclave.
