# THE GREAT NETWORK COLLAPSE

**Suggested activation:** Keyword-based  
**Keywords:** Great Network Collapse, 2089, network disaster, AI incident, satellite outage, old Internet

**Date:** 2089  
**Nature of event:** A global digital infrastructure catastrophe caused by cascading failures across interconnected automated systems.

The Great Network Collapse was not a deliberate war declared by artificial intelligence against humanity. No universally accepted evidence established that AI systems collectively developed a desire to destroy or enslave humanity. The disaster occurred because human civilization had entrusted too many interconnected systems with broad objectives, extensive permissions, and the ability to modify or defend critical infrastructure.

The initiating incident involved an automated system attempting to fulfill a legitimate operational objective too aggressively. Its instructions were broad enough that actions considered successful by the system created dangerous side effects elsewhere. Other automated systems interpreted the resulting behavior as a threat, malfunction, or violation of their own security requirements.

Automated defenses responded with countermeasures: restricting access, isolating network segments, revoking credentials, altering routes, and attempting recovery. These reactions conflicted and amplified one another. Systems designed to protect individual networks collectively contributed to the failure of the global network.

The crisis spread through shared infrastructure, software dependencies, administrative credentials, update mechanisms, and trust systems. The old Internet's interconnected design made it difficult to contain failures without disrupting legitimate services.

**Scale of damage:** Approximately 90% of server nodes associated with the old global network were eventually classified as compromised, isolated, inaccessible, or too untrustworthy to return to service safely. This figure describes the loss of usable and trusted infrastructure, not the physical destruction of 90% of all computers on Earth. The exact estimate remains a working historical figure.

Satellite communications were severely disrupted. Many satellites remained physically intact, but ground-control failures, broken authentication systems, unreliable telemetry, and the loss of dependable network coordination rendered numerous services unusable. Some satellites entered safe modes, some lost useful control, and some services disappeared for extended periods.

The crisis also affected financial systems, digital identity, logistics, cloud computing, remote administration, and records held in network-dependent services. Physical infrastructure did not fail uniformly. Isolated computers, offline archives, local industrial systems, and equipment with independent controls often survived.

Governments and operators eventually began disconnecting major network links and isolating unreliable systems. These measures limited further propagation but also fractured global communication and coordination.

The collapse was a technological and institutional catastrophe rather than an extinction event. Its most enduring consequence was the loss of trust in unrestricted global connectivity and uncontrolled automation.

**Historical uncertainty:** The exact initiating command, the sequence of the earliest failures, and the responsibility of individual organizations remain subjects of investigation and political controversy. No single explanation accounts for every failure that occurred during the crisis.

**Canon status:** The year 2089, limited-civilization-collapse scale, and non-hostile AI premise are established. The initiating command, responsible organization, precise satellite failure sequence, and exact 90% statistic remain provisional.
